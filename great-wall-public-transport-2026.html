Getting to the Great Wall Without a Tour

By Harris Wang · Xi’an since 2019 · updated November 2026

Almost every Great Wall article tells you which section is prettiest. Very few tell you how to physically get there without joining a coach group — and that is the part that decides your day, because the three main sections near Beijing are 60, 70 and 130 kilometres away, and only two of them have a scheduled public bus.

This is the transport side only: departure points, one-way fares, travel times, and what the day costs if you do it yourself instead of buying a tour. For the comparison of the sections themselves — walking lengths, crowds, cable cars, which one suits a family or a hiker — see the full Great Wall section comparison.

Two of those columns matter more than they look. The restored length tells you how long you can actually walk: 2.25 km at Mutianyu is a two-hour visit, 10 km at Jinshanling is a half-day hike. And “no scheduled service” is the reason Jinshanling quietly doubles the cost of a DIY day — you are pricing a car, not a bus ticket.

Badaling: the ¥12 bus from Deshengmen

Badaling is the cheapest wall to reach on your own. Buses leave from Deshengmen in north-central Beijing, take about an hour, cost ¥12 (roughly US$2) one way and run every 15 minutes between 06:00 and 12:00. There is no advance booking and no app: you queue, you board, you pay.

The trade-off is that Badaling is also the busiest section, the one every domestic tour group visits. Arriving on the first departures is not romantic advice — by mid-morning the approach ramps and the first watchtower are genuinely crowded, and the wall has no alternate route to spread people out.

Mutianyu: bus 867 express

Mutianyu has the better bus for anyone who wants a longer walk without hiring a car. The 867 express leaves at 08:00, takes about two hours and costs ¥30 (about US$4) one way. Add ¥45 entry and ¥120 (US$17) for the cable car return, and the transport-and-tickets side of the day lands around ¥225 — a figure that still undercuts the cheapest coach package.

The cable car is optional in theory and close to mandatory in practice: the alternative is a long stepped climb before you have walked a metre of wall. Mutianyu’s restored stretch is 2.25 km, which is why it works as a half-day rather than a full one.

Jinshanling and Gubeikou: where DIY stops working

Jinshanling is the section hikers ask about, and it is the one with no scheduled bus in any of our checks. The realistic options are a hired car at ¥900–1,200 (US$125–167) round trip, or a small-group tour with an English-speaking guide at ¥600–900 (US$83–125) per person.

What you get for that is the longest continuous walk of the three: about 10 km of wall, with 25 watchtowers on the Jinshanling–Simatai stretch, four to five hours one way. The Gubeikou–Jinshanling variant is 12 km and five to six hours, mixes restored and unrestored wall, and has almost no shade.

Cost of the same day, three ways. Bus to Badaling: ¥24 return plus ¥40 entry. Bus 867 to Mutianyu plus entry and cable car: about ¥225 all in. Day tour to Mutianyu (transport, entry, cable car, no guide): ¥350–500. Private car to Mutianyu: ¥600–800 return before tickets.

Tickets, passports and payment at the gate

Great Wall tickets are bought at the gate, and a passport is the document that gets you through the window as a foreign visitor. Payment is Alipay, WeChat Pay or cash. Two practical notes: carry about ¥200 in cash, because small vendors, some shuttle buses and the toboggan operator still prefer it; and expect water on the wall to cost ¥10–15 a bottle with no refill stations, so two litres per person is the working minimum.

Prices at the gate are not the only variable. Weekend pricing runs higher than weekday pricing at several sections, and Simatai’s cable car status changes with restoration work — check before you build a plan around it.

Timing the day

Both scheduled buses are morning services: the Deshengmen buses stop running at 12:00 and the 867 leaves at 08:00 only. That single fact rules out a lazy start. Leave on the first bus, walk for three to four hours, and you are back in Beijing by late afternoon; leave at ten and you spend the day in traffic instead of on the wall.

If your dates are flexible, the difference between a good and a bad Great Wall day is usually weather visibility rather than crowds — haze erases the ridgelines that make the section worth the trip. Transport questions that come up every time

Do I need to book Great Wall tickets in advance? No. Tickets are sold at the gate and a passport is the document that gets you through the window as a foreign visitor; payment is Alipay, WeChat Pay or cash. The booking problem foreign visitors hit in China is at museums and timed-entry sites, not at the wall.

Is the cable car included in the entry ticket? No, it is always separate. Mutianyu is ¥120 return on top of ¥45 entry; Badaling is ¥140 return on top of ¥40 peak-season entry; Jinshanling sells a one-way cable car at ¥80. Budget for it as a second ticket, not an add-on.

Can I visit two sections in one day? Not on public transport. The sections are 60, 70 and 130 km from Beijing and the buses are morning services — the Deshengmen departures stop at 12:00 and the 867 leaves at 08:00 only. Two sections in one day means a private car, and even then you spend four hours of it on the road.

What about the toboggan? Mutianyu and Badaling both run one, ¥100 each, downhill only. It is a fun way off the wall and a poor way up it.
