Chengdu Panda Base: An Early Morning Guide From a Repeat Visitor

I've been to the Chengdu Panda Base more times than I can count — I live in Xi'an and Chengdu is a two-hour high-speed train away — and I still think the first hour of the day decides the whole visit. Here's the short version for planning your own panda day.

Go early or watch sleeping cats

Pandas are crepuscular: most active at dawn, comatose by late morning. The base opens at 7:30, and if you're through the gate by 8:00 you'll see feeding time — cubs tumbling over each other, adults methodically demolishing bamboo. Arrive at 11:00 and you're looking at furry lumps on wooden platforms. This is not a maybe-tip; it is the single most important decision of the day.

The layout, in order

The base is bigger than most people expect. A workable loop: start at the Moon Nursery (cub enclosure — the biggest crowds form here later, so hit it first), walk up to the Adult Panda enclosures, then finish at the Red Panda forest, which most visitors skip. Red pandas are unfenced and half the fun. Allow 3-4 hours total including walking between enclosures.

Getting there and tickets

  • Metro Line 3 to Panda Avenue station, then the free shuttle or a short Didi ride — the metro + shuttle combo is the standard route and costs a few yuan.
  • Book tickets in advance through the official channels; same-day availability exists on weekdays but weekends can sell out, especially around Chinese holidays.
  • Skip private tours for this one: public transport is straightforward and the base is well-signposted in English.

The honest extras

The nursery has a glass walkway — keep quiet and don't tap. Summer visits need sunscreen and water; the shade is patchy. And if you want photos with adult pandas (holding one), that happens at a separate facility outside the base — research current rules before you go, they change.
