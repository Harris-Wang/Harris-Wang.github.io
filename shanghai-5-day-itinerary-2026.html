Shanghai in 5 Days: A Day-by-Day Plan

By Harris Wang · Xi’an since 2019 · updated November 2026

Five days is the sweet spot for Shanghai. Three days and you only see the postcard; seven and you start repeating yourself. Five lets you do the skyline, the old city, the former concession, one day out of town, and still keep a morning free for the thing most itineraries skip — eating.

This plan assumes you land at Pudong or Hongqiao, stay somewhere on Metro Line 2 or Line 10, and are travelling on the 240-hour visa-free transit or a standard tourist visa. If you are still working out whether the transit rule applies to your passport, the country list and port rules matter more than the itinerary does.

Day 1 — The Bund first, then the tower side

Do the Bund on your first afternoon even if you are tired. It is free, it needs no booking, and it reorients you: the river in front of you separates the 1920s financial district from the 1990s one, and everything else in the city makes more sense once you have seen both banks at once.

Walk the promenade from Yan’an Road north to Waibaidu Bridge, then take the sightseeing ferry across to Dongchang Road instead of the tourist cruise. It costs a couple of yuan, runs every few minutes, and drops you on the Pudong side for the Lujiazui loop: Shanghai Tower, the Oriental Pearl, and the elevated walkway that connects them.

Come back after dark. The Bund is a different place at 20:00, when the floodlights come on and the whole waterfront fills with people doing exactly what you are doing.

Day 2 — Old city, Yu Garden, and the alleys behind it

Yu Garden is a Ming-dynasty private garden begun in 1559, and it is genuinely worth the ticket — but go at opening, because by 10:30 the rockeries are a queue. The bazaar around it is a tourist market; the interesting food is one street further out, toward Fangbang Road, where the soup dumplings cost a third of the price and taste better.

After lunch, walk south to the City God Temple area, then take Line 10 two stops and switch to the former French Concession for the late afternoon: Wukang Road, Anfu Road, and the plane-tree streets between them. This is the part of Shanghai people move here for.

The Oriental Pearl Tower question. It is in Lujiazui, on the Pudong side of the river — the spiky pink-and-grey tower you can see from the Bund. It is not on the Bund itself, and it is not the tallest thing in the cluster (Shanghai Tower, at 632 m, is the third-tallest building in the world). If you want one observation deck, take Shanghai Tower for the height and the view down; take the Oriental Pearl if you want the glass floor and the slightly retro experience of a 1990s icon. Do not do both on the same day.

Day 3 — Museums, West Bund, or a slow morning

Shanghai’s museums are free or nearly free and almost all of them now require a timed reservation made a few days ahead — the Shanghai Museum and the Shanghai Museum East both fill up, especially at weekends. Book before you fly, not on the morning.

If contemporary art is more your thing, take Line 11 to the West Bund: Long Museum, Tank Shanghai, and the riverfront path between them make a half-day that does not feel like a museum day at all. Finish with dinner in Xintiandi or, cheaper and better, on Yongkang Road.

Day 4 — One day out: Suzhou, or a water town

Two options, and the choice is about pace rather than quality.

OptionGetting thereBest for
Suzhou (gardens + canals)High-speed rail from Hongqiao, roughly 25 minutesGardens, silk, a real city with its own food
Zhujiajiao water townMetro Line 17 to Zhujiajiao, about an hour door to doorCanals without a train ticket; easy half-day

Suzhou rewards an early start: the Humble Administrator’s Garden is at its best before 09:00, and the Pingjiang Road lanes are empty for about an hour after that. Zhujiajiao is the lower-effort choice and works well if you are travelling with children or on a short sleep deficit.

Either way, book the return train the same day you book the outbound — Friday and Sunday evening services from Suzhou do sell out.

Day 5 — Disney, or the city without a plan

Shanghai Disneyland is in Chuansha, in Pudong New Area — not downtown, and not next to the airport. Metro Line 11 runs to Disney Resort station, and the trip from People’s Square is around 50 minutes. If that is your plan for day five, buy a dated ticket in advance and be at the gate before opening; the first two hours are worth more than the last four.

If it is not, spend the last day on the things five-day itineraries always cut: the Propaganda Poster Art Centre, a long lunch, Jing’an Temple, and one last walk along the river after dark. Then take the maglev to Pudong airport if you are flying out of PVG — 30 km in about eight minutes, and the only airport transfer in the world that is also a mild attraction.

Practical notes that change the trip

  • Payments. Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with your own card before you arrive; cash works but small vendors increasingly do not keep change.
  • Metro. The system is signed in English, costs almost nothing, and beats taxis in traffic. Lines 2 and 10 cover most of this itinerary.
  • Bookings. Museums and Disney need timed reservations; gardens and the Bund do not.
  • Weather. June to September is hot and humid with real rain; December to February is cold and grey but the city is quiet and hotel rates drop.

If you only have five days, this is the order that works

Bund and Lujiazui, old city, concession and museums, one day out, Disney or nothing. It front-loads the things that need good weather and leaves the flexible day at the end, which is where plans usually break.
